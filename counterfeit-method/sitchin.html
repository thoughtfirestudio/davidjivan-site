the counterfeit method · 01

Sitchin sounds like a scholar.
That's the move.

You watched the documentary. Or you read the book. Or somebody you respect wouldn't shut up about it, so you went and looked, and what you found wasn't the easy hand-wave you'd been promised by the people who told you not to bother. Sitchin doesn't sound like a crank. He sounds like a Sumerologist. He pulls up cuneiform. He gives you transliterations. He tells you what the texts actually say versus what the textbooks claim they say. He invokes a suppressed body of knowledge and presents himself as the person willing to read it straight.

So the question forms — and it is the right question to ask — is this serious?

The short answer is no. The longer answer is the one worth your time, because most people who say no are saying no for the wrong reason. The standard debunker move is to point at the conclusions and laugh. Anunnaki. Nibiru. Ancient astronauts. Obviously not. That move tells you nothing. Weird conclusions are not, by themselves, disqualifying — Origen reached strange conclusions through transparent, rigorous method, and Origen is one of the most serious theologians of the early Logos tradition. The conclusions are not where Sitchin fails. The method is where Sitchin fails. And the failure has a specific shape, because the method is performed, not actual.

That is what this installment is about: pseudo-Assyriology as a worked example. The apparatus of expertise — the transliterations, the source-comparison, the "what the texts really say" — used to license conclusions the actual field rejects. Not because the field is closed-minded. Because the moves don't survive scrutiny once you know what to look at. The transliteration that gets quoted is contested. The "most accurate rendering" is one rendering, presented as the only one. A philological judgment-call carries the weight of a settled fact. Repeat that fifty times across a book and you have a feeling of scholarship without the substance of it.

I want to be careful here. The point of this piece isn't Sitchin. The point is the pattern — the structural moves that make a piece of writing sound like recovered scholarship when it's actually invention dressed in scholarship's clothes. Once you can see those moves, you stop needing somebody else to tell you what's serious and what isn't. You can run the test yourself. On Sitchin, yes. On the next book, the next podcast, the next thread. That's the skill. The Sitchin case is just where we sharpen it.

if this is you
  • You watched the documentary, or fell into the YouTube hole, and walked out thinking that can't be right — but I can't actually say why.
  • The "ancient aliens are obviously stupid" crowd lost you years ago. The contempt is louder than the argument, and you noticed.
  • You read The Twelfth Planet and found it unexpectedly impressive — the philological apparatus, the cuneiform, the confidence — and you've been quietly unsure where that leaves you.
  • You suspect mainstream gatekeeping is real and you suspect a lot of "they don't want you to know" content is invented. You don't currently have a way to tell which is which.
  • You don't want to be the person who falls for it. You also don't want to be the person who reflexively dismisses anything outside the consensus.
  • You've started to notice that the feeling of expertise can be performed, and you want a sharper way to spot the performance.
what this installment includes
The case
A method critique of The Twelfth Planet — not a chapter-by-chapter takedown. The specific structural moves Sitchin uses to convert contested philology into load-bearing conclusion, walked through carefully so you can see them happening.
The test
The demarcation tool, applied in real time. The difference between recovering what a source actually says and reading backward from a desired conclusion — named, demonstrated, and used on the Sumerian and Akkadian material Sitchin built his career on.
The contrast
Origen as the positive case. A short tour of what rigorous heterodox recovery actually looks like — reaching strange and suppressed conclusions through honest method. Archaeology vs. invention. The distinction is the whole game.
The skill
The transferable pattern. What you walk away with isn't Sitchin debunked and the end. It's the structural eye — applicable to anything that performs the apparatus of expertise to license a conclusion the experts reject. Once you have it, you can't un-have it.
