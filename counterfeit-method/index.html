method course

Some scholarship recovers what's real.
Some performs the appearance of it.

There's a kind of work — in religion, in alternative history, in spirituality — that wears the apparatus of rigor. Transliterations. Source comparisons. Phrases like most accurate rendering. The footnote count is high. The conclusions feel like recovered truth that someone, somewhere, didn't want you to have.

I've spent a lot of time inside this material. Some of it is real. Some of it is dressed-up invention. Telling them apart is a skill, and almost nobody teaches it.

The mechanism is this: a writer performs the appearance of method to license conclusions the actual field rejects. The performance is what does the work. Strip the apparatus and the argument is just a story; with the apparatus, it reads as recovered scholarship. That move — the performance of method standing in for method — is what this series is about.

This is not a debunker blog. I'm not interested in laughing at people who got pulled in by Sitchin or ancient astronauts or sensational Gnosticism cosplay. I was one of them. The curiosity that takes those books seriously is the right response to a world where institutions hide more than they say. The problem was never the curiosity. The problem was the supply. What I want to give you is the tool to keep the curiosity and stop getting conned by it.

  • You want real heterodox scholarship to exist — because some genuine things were suppressed — and you're tired of the baby getting thrown out with the bathwater every time someone gets caught dressing invention up as recovery.
  • You don't want to be told the answer. You want the tools to read it yourself.
what the course teaches
Demarcation
The archaeology-vs-invention test. A clean, portable rule for telling work that follows the sources forward from work that reads backward from a desired conclusion. Once you have it, you stop needing experts to vouch for things — you can see the move yourself.
The ladder
Established, defensible, speculative. The three categories pseudo-scholarship is built to blur. Most counterfeit method works by collapsing the ladder so a speculative claim inherits the authority of an established one. Recognize the move and the whole apparatus comes apart in your hands.
Worked example
Installment 01 — Sitchin's The Twelfth Planet. The inaugural case. Not a chapter-by-chapter debunk. A close look at how the argument is built — the philological theater, the transliteration sleight-of-hand, the structural moves — so the next time you meet that shape, you recognize it.
The positive case
What real heterodox recovery looks like. Origen reached conclusions the church later condemned, through transparent method that did the work. The contrast — archaeology vs. invention — is what the whole skill turns on. Future installments will work other examples in religion, alt-history, and spirituality.
