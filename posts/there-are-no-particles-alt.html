There Are No Particles

David Jivan · davidjivan.net

There are no particles.

Not "particles are weird." Not "particles behave like waves sometimes." The claim of quantum field theory — the most rigorously tested physical theory in history — is that what we call particles do not exist as fundamental objects. An electron is not a tiny ball. It is an excitation of the electron field, which fills all of space, all the time. The photon is a ripple in the electromagnetic field. Every physical thing you have ever touched is, at bottom, a pattern of excitation in underlying fields.

The Standard Model names seventeen of them. The deeper question — whether those are seventeen independent fields or seventeen aspects of one field — is open at the frontier of physics. But the direction of travel is not ambiguous. From solid matter to atoms to subatomic particles to fields to possibly one field. The substrate is unified, or trending that way.

This is not mysticism. This is what the equations say.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

The strange part is not the physics. The strange part is how precisely this maps onto a tradition that had no access to the physics.

Heraclitus, around 500 BCE: the Logos is the rational principle underlying all change. Everything flows, but not randomly. The deep order makes the flux coherent.

The Stoics, three centuries later: the Logos is the active, rational, fiery principle pervading all of nature. Individual human reason participates in the cosmic Logos. They were not doing metaphor. They were making a claim about the structure of reality.

Philo of Alexandria, a Jewish philosopher working between the testaments: the Logos as intermediary between the transcendent and the created, the rational principle through which the world is ordered.

Then John, opening his gospel not with a birth narrative but with a cosmological claim: "In the beginning was the Logos... All things came into being through it, and apart from it not one thing came into being that came into being."

John 1:3 and QFT are making the same structural claim at the same level: the rational ordering principle is the condition for anything physical to exist. The Logos tradition says nothing physical arises apart from the Logos. QFT says nothing physical arises apart from the field. Strip both down to their actual claims and they point at the same structure.

The convergence is real. It is also not proof of anything theological. Physics does not reach God. What it does do — unexpectedly — is converge with the Logos tradition's claim about the lawful, rational substrate of physical reality. That convergence is worth taking seriously. It is not identity.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Here is where precision matters, and where a lot of well-meaning writing goes wrong.

The New Testament has two different claims about the Logos, and they point at two different levels. "All things came into being through the Logos" (John 1:3) is a claim about the ordering principle of physical reality — what QFT describes. "In him we live and move and have our being" (Acts 17:28) is a claim about Being itself, the ground of awareness, the in-which any appearing happens at all. That is not what physics describes. Physics stops well before it reaches the ground of consciousness.

The field does not exhaust existence. That claim follows from the consciousness-first commitment — the position that consciousness is the irreducible given, not a late product of neural complexity — not from QFT itself. The field is the deepest layer of the render, not the ground in which the render appears. Space is itself a field-structure. The ground is prior to it, not located relative to it.

Fusing these two levels is the most common error in this territory. It makes the convergence look like proof-texting — as though physics has finally caught up with theology and validated it. That is not what is happening. What is happening is that two independent inquiries into the structure of reality have produced maps that match at one level and diverge at another. The match is real. The divergence is also real, and it matters.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

The place where materialism runs out of rope is not the field. It is consciousness.

The hard problem — why there is subjective experience at all, why there is something it is like to be you rather than just information processing happening in the dark — does not close from the materialist side. Every proposed solution explains how information gets processed. None explain why any of it is experienced. The explanatory gap deepens the more carefully you look.

If consciousness is fundamental — the ground, not the product — then the field is render, appearing within that ground. The brain does not generate consciousness; it is how consciousness appears from within a localized perspective of its own rendering. This is not a denial of science. Science is the systematic study of the structure of the render, and that structure is real and rigorous. It is a denial that the render is the outer limit of what exists.

The measurement problem in quantum mechanics is genuinely unresolved. What counts as observation? What causes the wave function to collapse? The standard Copenhagen interpretation invokes something like an observer without cleanly defining what an observer is. The von Neumann/Wigner interpretation makes consciousness the cause of collapse — not a fringe position, but one held by a founder of quantum mechanics. I don't hang the theological position on this. But it is worth noting: the place where quantum mechanics gets philosophically strange is exactly the place where consciousness-first positions become most plausible.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

What becomes available when you hold both claims seriously — the QFT convergence at the level of physical structure, and the consciousness-first commitment at the level of ground — is a framework in which the Logos tradition is not a pre-scientific guess but a structurally precise description of reality at the level it was actually aiming at.

Origen of Alexandria, the third-century theologian who built the most ambitious Christian metaphysics before it was condemned and buried, understood the Logos as the rational principle through which all things exist — not primarily a title for Jesus, but the eternal ordering structure of which Jesus was the supreme human expression. He was pointing at the level of render-order. The ground of Being, what Acts 17:28 names, was for him the prior in-which that the Logos itself was not other than. He had the distinction, even if he didn't have the vocabulary of quantum fields.

The tradition was more precise than it is usually given credit for. And it was suppressed not because it was sloppy but because it was dangerous — a theology in which all things subsist in one ground and return to it has no use for eternal damnation as an enforcement mechanism. The condemnation was political. The ideas survived in the margins.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

I don't know what to do with all of this except keep thinking it through. The convergence between QFT and the Logos tradition is real at the level of physical structure and stops before it reaches the ground of Being. That stopping point is not a failure. It is a precision. Physics describes the render. The Logos tradition maps the render's rational order — and, through the apophatic tradition, points toward what the render appears within.

The field does not exhaust existence. The question is what else there is, and whether the traditions that have been pointing at it for twenty-five centuries are doing something more than poetry.

I think they are. I'm still working out what that means.
