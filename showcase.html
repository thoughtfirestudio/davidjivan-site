The fork underneath everything
The hinge: a being, or Being
Reading A
God as a being

The supreme entity — most powerful, most present. A thing among things, first in rank. Infinity is a possession God holds in the largest measure.

Reading B
God as Being

Not the largest item but the ground of there being items at all — the in-which. Infinity isn't a quantity God has more of. God isn't on the quantity-scale to begin with.

Every round of the argument turns on this one fork.

cards
Grid of cards for thinkers, sources, or parallel claims. Hover lifts. Auto-fits 1-3 columns.
Aristotle
4th c. BCE
potential vs. actual

The number series is potentially infinite — there is no largest number. "Numbers are limited" fails before theology even starts. (He still denied a completed actual infinite — a claim about math, not about God.)

Aquinas
13th c.
intensive vs. extensive

God's infinity is unbounded perfection — plenitude of being — explicitly not infinity of magnitude. Two different predicates. (He himself doubted a completed actual infinite in creation.)

Cantor
19th c.
Absolute vs. transfinite

The Absolute Infinite is unincreasable and resists determination. The transfinite is real, graded, and increasable. Two categorically different things wearing one word.

timeline
Vertical timeline for chronological sequences. Active items glow green. For lineage, exchange, or historical progressions.
~500 BCE
Heraclitus

Names the Logos — the rational principle underneath all change. The deep order that makes the flux intelligible.

3rd c. BCE
The Stoics

Systematize the Logos as the active, rational force pervading all matter. Coin logos spermatikos — seeds of reason in every mind.

~25 BCE - 50 CE
Philo of Alexandria

The hinge figure. Synthesizes Stoic Logos with the God of Israel. The Logos as God's firstborn — the bridge between the transcendent and the manifest.

~90-100 CE
The Fourth Gospel

In the beginning was the Logos. Takes the full tradition and makes the incarnation claim: the ordering principle became flesh.

~184-253 CE
Origen of Alexandria

Builds the first complete Christian metaphysics. Origin, fall, incarnation, purification, return. Condemned 300 years after his death.

thesis
Big centered quote for the core claim. Use at the end of a section or as a standalone punctuation moment. <em> highlights in green.
"

The Logos was toward God, and what God was, the Logos was.

This sentence encodes, in a single line of Greek, a relationship that the tradition would spend three centuries trying to formalize. Distinction without separation. Intimacy without identity.

Info or caveat box. Use for honest caveats, sourcing notes, or asides that shouldn't interrupt the argument. Add callout--accent for a green left border.
Honest caveat. Cantor himself did not think the universe was spatially infinite. He's company for "real infinity doesn't crowd God" — not a witness for "the cosmos is infinite." That second question is physics, and physics keeps its own counsel.
Source note. Primary source: the Cantor-Franzelin correspondence (1885-86), in Georg Cantor: Briefe, Meschkowski & Nilson, eds. (Springer, 1991).
